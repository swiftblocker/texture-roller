# S026: Make your own Texture Roller for CHEAP! (Rae'chel Does Wonders)

- Type: youtube

## Key claims
- Handle core: dollar-store wooden dowel/handle, uncoated, cut to about 5 in (127 mm). Leaving it longer gives grip room during work.
- Clay: Sculpey polymer clay (waterproof, oven-cured), rolled to about 2 mm thick, at least 5 in long, wrapped with overlap. Pasta roller on largest setting used.
- Clay must be thick enough: too-thin clay (first attempt) gave shallow impressions.
- Stones washed first (sand granules). Cornstarch dusted on clay reduces stickiness.
- Impressions pressed deep but not puncturing clay. Trim wrap edges and thin them for overlap.
- Bake per label with clay not touching the pan (hang the roller or prop the handle).
- Ends capped with two-part epoxy putty after baking. Alternatively, polymer end cap pre-bake. Wood handle wedged with paper towel after clay loosened.
- Walls of a separate tile set used silicone (not polymer) on foam; $7 tube lasted about 30 tiles. Grid sizes: 1 in grid, walls 2 in tall, trunks 4 in.
- Paint: half matte mod podge + gray acrylic base, dry-brush tan/golden brown, dark wash, final dry-brush.
- No 3D printing or relief depth in mm given.
