# Clay behavior: sticking, tearing, release agents, clay state, shrinkage, glaze pooling

"(weak)" = only trust 1-2 sources back the claim.

## Sticking and release agents
- A printed roller sticks to clay without a release agent such as baby powder; one test says printed stamps stuck even with baby powder on Model Magic [S020].
- Light cornstarch dusting recommended for a printed roller [S050]; cornstarch also reduces stickiness on polymer clay [S026]; dust with cornstarch or roll leather-hard clay [S100].
- Powdered sugar used when dough sticks (cookie use) [S104].
- Oils: WD-40 or vegetable oil worked into grooves with a toothbrush gives more detail on release [S081]; cooking oil spray on a linoleum mat, may be unnecessary if clay is not tacky and the design is open [S082].
- Clay sticks to PVC more than wood, so wood is preferred for a hand roller [S049, S069, S088].
- Bevelled edges give clean release from clay [S043, S047].
- Slightly moisten clay (vendor advice, no numbers) [S067] (weak).
- PLA is fine; PETG copes with warm water [S100].
- Peel a mat from one corner or halfway from each side [S082].
- No source tests oil vs powder vs wax on a printed roller or on a given clay.
- Release agent not mentioned for clay-built rollers [S080, S084].

## Tearing and distortion
- Press slab down from outside edges toward centre so clay fills grooves; flip so the slab weight releases it [S081].
- Even pressure gives predictable results; rotate the roller or change where each pass starts and ends to reduce repetition [S049, S088].
- Rolling needs little pressure; no glove needed [S020].
- Handles that are too thick can jam against the clay and stop the roller turning [S022].
- Too soft clay distorts; too firm may not take an impression [S086] (weak).
- Model Magic and putty warp; attach the slab to a surface quickly [S020].
- Dry clay cracks the mat and the cracks show in the impression [S082].
- No source reports tearing measurements or undercut limits [S080, S083, S084].

## Clay state
- Rolled clay stages used by potters: very wet for initial transfer; slightly soft for carving; leatherhard for drying/smoothing; bone dry for refining; soft clay for test-rolling a finished stamp [S084].
- Texture work best between soft and leather-hard [S086] (weak); slabs rolled/stamped while soft and used immediately [S080].
- Freshly rolled slab used with a hand roller [S049].
- Leather hard is roughly 12 to 14% moisture, where wet-to-dry shrinkage stops [S106] (weak).
- Moisture: slip-cast about 28-30%; extruded about 19-22% [S106] (weak).
- Over-bend slabs over a large-diameter pipe because they like to open up as they dry; score, slip and compress joins with rib or brayer [S068, S080]. Pipe size not given.
- Transfer slabs to cement board before texturing [S080].

## Shrinkage (fired)
- Earthenware 5-10% [S106] (weak); 6-8% to cone 06 [S107] (weak).
- Stoneware 10-15% or more [S106] (weak); 11-13% between cone 6 and cone 9 [S107] (weak); about 12.5% working rate [S109] (weak).
- Porcelain 14-15% to cone 9 [S107] (weak); porcelain shrinks more due to feldspar, not whiteness [S087].
- Fired shrinkage curve typically peaks below 15%; one body shrinks almost 17% wet to fired; a body at cone 2 under 6%; cone 04 about 2% with low strength [S108].
- Laguna cone 6 (retailer-reproduced): white stoneware 13% (abs 1%); red #80 14.0% (abs 0.5%); red #90 13.0% (abs 0.9%); redstone cone 5-6 10.5% (abs 0.5%); paper clay cone 06-10 14% +/-2% [S105] (weak).
- Conflict: stoneware 10-15% [S106] vs 11-13% [S107] vs 12.5% [S109]; consistent only as a range of about 10-15%.
- No source gives drying (wet-to-bone-dry) shrinkage % [S087, S108, S106].
- Implication (derived, not stated): relief and pattern size shrink by the same percent; no source applies this to rollers.

## Glaze pooling and firing
- Carved stamps reverse the imprint; potter must decide which areas are raised (catch glaze) and which recessed (glaze pools) [S080].
- Clay bodies soften near their melting point in firing; porcelain most susceptible [S087].
- Relevant to texture rounding during firing; no depth figure [S087].
- Glaze fit: more silica in the body improves fit; 20-25% silica recommended [S087].
- Thicker walls and wider, deeper foot ring reduce warping [S087].
- Slip breaks over texture and shows body through [S085].
- Wax resist at leather-hard before dipping [S085].
