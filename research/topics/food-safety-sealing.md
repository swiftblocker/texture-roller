# Food safety and sealing

"(weak)" = only trust 1-2 sources back the claim. Both food-safety sources are generic 3D-printing articles; neither covers clay contact. The roller touches clay, not food, but pottery for dishes is the end use, so rule judgment is open.

## Printed plastic
- FDM leaves grooves between layers; groove depth grows with layer height; use the lowest practical layer height for food contact [S070].
- Bacteria in the grooves is the main FDM concern [S070]; pores between layers trap moisture and particles; the article treats PLA utensils as single use [S071].
- Pure PLA considered food-safe (cited 1995 GRAS study); coloured PLA may carry non-food-safe dyes or additives; prefer undyed and check the SDS [S071].
- Use a stainless steel nozzle, not brass (lead) [S070, S071].
- PLA softens and distorts around 60-70 C; not dishwasher safe; not for hot liquids [S070]; cannot be boiled or dishwashed [S071].
- Bleach and microwaving do not sanitise printed parts [S070].

## Sealing and coatings
- Food-grade epoxy, polyurethane or FDA-approved PTFE applied by dip coating [S070].
- Epoxy must be FDA-certified food-safe and fully cured; liquid epoxy is toxic (gloves, mask) [S071].
- One cited test: epoxy-coated prints showed no bacterial growth after 14 days of use and washing; uncoated PLA showed significant growth [S071].
- Silicone seals pores and is less hazardous than epoxy [S071].
- Chemical smoothing (acetone, d-limonene, ethyl acetate) possible but a food-safe coating is still recommended [S070].
- Coatings can degrade or wear and may not be dishwasher safe [S070, S071].
- Two-part epoxy putty used to cap the ends of a polymer roller [S026].

## Roller materials and finishes in the market
- Wood rollers: "food-safe finish" [S042]; protective oil finish, usable with dough, fondant or clay [S044]; tung oil [S048].
- Cookie rollers (3D printed) share designs with clay rollers; no food-safe advice given [S103, S104].
- Hot cornstarch/oil release agents are food-adjacent but not evaluated [S050, S081, S082].
- Plaster roller dust must be cleaned off so chips do not contaminate clay [S083].
- Polymer clay is waterproof and plastic-based [S026].
- PLA washing guidance: PLA fine, PETG copes with warm water [S100].

## Open questions
- Is any sealing needed when the printed roller only touches raw clay that is later fired?
- Does the roller transfer PLA particles, oil or dye into clay bodies that will later hold food?
- Which release agents are safe for clay that becomes tableware?
- Recommended cleaning method between clay bodies (stoneware vs porcelain contamination).
- Does epoxy coating survive wet clay abrasion?
